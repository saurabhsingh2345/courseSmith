# L28 — "Day 5: Karpathy on Vibe Coding & Rules for Building a Commercial MVP" (10:35)

**Slides only.** Opens the final day of Week 1 — another **blue day**. "This week is
meant to be about vibe coding for fun and profit. We've done plenty of fun; it's
time to look at the profit side."

He also names the day's contract: yesterday he taught the rules and then broke them
all by YOLOing. **Today he follows them.**

## Part 1 — the Karpathy tweets in chronological order (0:30-7:00)

Three posts, deliberately ordered by date, two of which we have already seen:

**1. February last year — coining vibe coding.** Give in fully to the vibes,
embrace the exponentials, forget the code exists; possible because the LLMs are
getting too good. Karpathy names **Cursor Composer and Sonnet**.

**2. December — the "alien tool" post** that opened the course (see `ref-L03.md`).

**3. End of January — a long, unstructured train of thought**, which is the new
material and the meat of the lecture. He walks its sections:

| section | what Karpathy says |
|---|---|
| **Coding workflow** | He has gone from **80% manual autocomplete / 20% agents** to **80% agents / 20% edits and touching up** |
| **Agent swarms** | "The no-need-for-IDE-anymore hype and the agent-swarm hype is too much for me right now. The models definitely make mistakes. If you have code you actually care about, **I would watch them like a hawk**." Ed: "I could not agree more" — the 2026 movement is ahead of its time for serious work |
| **Tenacity** | Agents **work relentlessly — never tired, never demoralised, they just keep going** |
| **Speed-ups** | He can't measure it. Feels net way faster, but **the main effect is that he does a lot more than he was going to do.** "A speed-up, but possibly a lot more about an expansion" |
| **Leverage** | Bigger goals, reachable by putting it in a loop — the power of the loop |
| **Fun** | Unexpected: agent programming feels **more** fun, because it takes the drudgery and the head-against-wall moments. **LLM coding splits engineers** — and Karpathy suspects that maps onto **people who like coding versus people who like building** |
| **Atrophy** | He is **losing technical acumen**, falling out of practice. Ed says he feels it too — "the temptation just to constantly fire back at the agents", and **friction when he has to go back and read code** |
| **"Slop apocalypse"** | Bracing for the year LLM slop goes everywhere. Ed links it to the Jellyfin policy from L24 and predicts a **backlash** — a movement toward code that is visibly not slop |

## Part 2 — the deliberate repeat (7:00-10:35)

He flags it up front: **"I'm going to do what I sometimes do, which is annoying of
me"** — repeat yesterday's rules, fast, because they matter.

**BE THE BOSS**, compressed: invest in `agents.md` (spec / style / success); start
simple and work incrementally, testing constantly; **don't get lazy — challenge,
demand evidence**, or as Karpathy says, watch it like a hawk; and **handle
frustration** — when you hit the same problem repeatedly, step back and do it
differently.

Then the two audiences again, with **one genuinely new and much better analogy**
for the junior half:

> Treat it as if **you are a non-technical manager put in charge of people who are
> very enthusiastic and eager but not always right** — they jump to conclusions and
> trip over each other. Your job is to be **the probing manager**: give me the
> evidence, have you thought about this, show me, explain this to me. Including the
> classic **"this might be a silly question, but…"** followed by a pointed one.

For seniors: **less coding, more building** — "this is the new craft".

Closes on the balanced attitude: expect mixed results, don't buy the 10x, **you
cannot sit back and YOLO your way to a complete product** — think that and you'll
hit a wall. Work incrementally and you'll love it.
