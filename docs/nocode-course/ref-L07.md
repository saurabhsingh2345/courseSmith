# L7 — "Day 1: The 8 Stages of AI Coding: From ChatGPT to Agent Orchestration" (10:31)

Slides throughout. Two distinct halves: the eight stages (~6 min), then AI costs
(~4½ min).

## Part 1 — the eight stages (Steve Yegge)

Credited to a popular post by **Steve Yegge**, author of **Gastown** (covered later
in the course). Yegge generated an illustration per stage with **Gemini's Nano
Banana**; he shows those images.

| stage | what it is |
|---|---|
| 1 | ChatGPT and AI autocomplete — something you refer over to |
| 2 | Coding agent in the **IDE sidebar**, asking permission before every change |
| 3 | Same sidebar, but **YOLO mode** — accept everything, go for it |
| 4 | Agent occupies the **main window**. Your attention is on the agent, not the code; diffs fly past unapproved |
| 5 | Moved to the **CLI** — Claude Code or similar, still YOLO, diffs scrolling unchecked. Walk away, come back, it's done |
| 6 | **Multiple agents** concurrently — a little team of three to five |
| 7 | **10+ agents**, all coordinated by you, by hand |
| 8 | **An agent orchestrating the other agents** — hierarchies, manager agents, worker agents, a testing team, a design team |

Then the mapping onto the course, which is the load-bearing part:

- **Week 1 → stages 2, 3, 4** (stage 1 assumed)
- **Week 2 → stage 5**, dug into deeply
- **Week 3 → stages 6, 7, 8**
- His own verdict: **stages 5-6 are the sweet spot** for real enterprise software.
  7 and 8 are shown so you have seen them; whether you'd use them in production
  today is "an open question"

## Part 2 — AI costs (from ~6:00)

| beat | what he says |
|---|---|
| the reassurance | **You can take the entire course without spending a penny** on API costs |
| the other side | You can also burn a great deal — 20 agents building something enormous will happily take your money. He does, and enjoys it |
| his recommendation | Cursor's free trial while eligible, then the **$20/month Claude Code plan** for weeks 2 and 3. Beyond that only if you want intensity |
| the free path | Resources carry tips for free models; some paid products are shown "for future reference" only, and you pick and choose |
| **the golden rule** | He takes responsibility for getting you able to answer Karpathy's tweet. He explicitly **does not take responsibility for your AI spend** — pricing varies by region and offer. You monitor it, you decide |
| the reframe | A $1,000 laptop doesn't shock us; lots of $20/month subscriptions do. Hundreds of trillions of floating-point operations, someone pays the electricity |
| the close | "You're in charge of your charges" — but ask him anything |
