# Capstone

## What we are building
- A small tool that a real person could use on Monday: a request tracker.
  Somebody submits a request, you see the queue, you mark things done.
- Every layer from lesson two appears exactly once. Nothing new is introduced.
- The point is not the app. It is watching the parts connect at full speed.

## Step one: the brief
- Who it is for, what they do with it, what it must remember, how it should feel.
- Four sentences, written before anything is opened.
- If you cannot write them, the idea is not ready and no tool will rescue that.

[DIAGRAM: the-whole-flow]

## Step two: build the first version
- Straight into a builder, brief pasted in, one screen out.
- Do not fix anything yet. Look at the whole thing first and write down what is
  wrong, in order of how much it matters.

## Step three: the data
- Two tables: requests, and the people who made them.
- Draw it before you ask for it. Four minutes on paper.

## Step four: make it look chosen
- One typeface, one accent, more space than feels necessary, one idea per screen.
- Ten minutes. The difference is not subtle.

## Step five: take ownership and ship
- The project becomes files you keep, in version control, deployed to an address.
- Watch this happen for real — including the waiting.

[CAPTURE: tool=claude, fixture=habit-tracker; take the project from a working state to a committed, deploy-ready one, showing what changed at each step]

## Step six: watch somebody use it
- Hand it to one person and say nothing.
- Every place they hesitate is a defect, whatever you intended.
- This step is skipped more than any other and it is the only one that tells you
  the truth.

## What you actually walk away with
- The files, which you keep whatever happens to any company involved.
- The data, exported once so you know you can.
- An address other people can open.
- The habit of describing things precisely — which is the transferable part and
  will outlast every tool named in this course.

## Where to go now
- Build the second thing. It takes a fraction of the time and that is the
  moment this becomes a skill rather than a course you finished.
- Come back to lesson twelve before you promise anybody anything.
